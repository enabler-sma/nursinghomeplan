💬 My current condition - for the nursing home to evaluate if I'm eligible to enrol to their home

I am 40 years old and living with Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA) — a condition that affects the muscles needed for movement. I am unable to perform any of the 6 Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) on my own, which means I need assistance with bathing, dressing, feeding, toileting, transferring, and walking. I use a powered wheelchair to get around.

My mum has been my primary caregiver for many years, and she is getting older. I am planning ahead so that if something happens to her suddenly, there is a safe and dignified place for me to go — without panic, and without waiting.

I need your help visiting nursing homes and finding out whether they are suitable. There are two key scenarios I am preparing for:

🚨
Urgent Respite Stay
If my mum falls sick suddenly and cannot care for me, I need a place that can admit me quickly — possibly within the same day.
🏠
Long-Term Stay
When my mum can no longer provide regular care, I will need a nursing home that can support my needs on an ongoing, permanent basis.
⚠️
Important Info: Most nursing homes in Singapore only accept elderly residents. The typical minimum age is 65. As I am only 40+, many facilities may turn me away outright, or accept me only on a case-by-case basis.
🎯
Your mission: Visit the nursing homes and use the evaluation checklist I've prepared to record what you find out. Look not just at what they say, but what you see and feel. I want to find a home where the conditions are as good as they can be — not just functional, but genuinely liveable. The checklist covers admissions, medical support, hygiene, environment, food, dignity, and end-of-life care. I am especially counting on your honest sensory impressions of each place.

🔒 Loss of Freedom & Choice
A position of submission
Anita describes moving into a nursing home as immediately losing control over one's own life. 03:35
Controlled schedules
Every part of the day — eating, resting, sleeping — is dictated by staff. Residents are expected to be in bed by 8:00 PM. 38:08 12:28
Loss of movement
Most residents spend their entire day in a wheelchair or lying in bed. Any attempt to move independently is often met with "Where are you going?" — well-intentioned, but diminishing to one's natural instincts. 38:13 38:55
Dehumanization
Anita describes the experience as being "dehumanized" — her entire existence reduced to a bed, a bedside table, and meals. 02:44 06:18
🏠 Living Conditions & Environment
Ward living — too close for comfort
Anita stays in a six-bedded ward, describing it as "too much human body and heat" — difficult to endure for extended periods. 01:04:40 01:05:04
Dementia ward — dim and enclosed
The high-dependency dementia ward is darker and more enclosed. Anita notes a lack of positive lighting, which she believes contributes to exhaustion and depression. 22:08 22:35
Overwhelming sensory environment
The space is filled with constant noise and "brutal" sensory experiences — the smell of waste, rows of beds, and little relief from stimulation. 17:31
"Functional food" — nutrition without joy
Meals are packet-based slop — Anita describes the texture as "thick saliva." There is no taste, no smell, no pleasure in eating. 01:02:14 34:26
Institutional, not home
Anita states that 70–80% of the environment has "nothing homely about it" — designed for functionality, not for living. 21:42 01:05:10
💭 Reflections on Aged Care

Anita concludes that the current system prioritises efficiency and "functionality" over the humanity of residents. Feeding and medication are the focus — quality of life is an afterthought.

Hopefully we could find a place where I can live comfortably in a condusive environment. Thank you!
